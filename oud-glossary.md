# Oud glossary (quick terms)

Short definitions for shoppers exploring oud and Gulf fragrance. For a fuller primer, see [What is oud?](https://uaeperfume.ae/blogs/guides/what-is-oud) and [Emirati perfume culture](https://uaeperfume.ae/blogs/guides/emirati-perfume-culture).

## Core terms

| Term | Plain meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Oud / agarwood** | Resinous wood from *Aquilaria* trees that formed after infection; prized for a deep, woody-animalic scent. |
| **Oud oil (dehn al oud)** | Distilled oil from agarwood chips; concentration and origin drive price and character. |
| **Bakhoor** | Scented wood chips or blocks burned on charcoal (or electric burners) to fragrance clothes and rooms. |
| **Mukhallat** | A blended oil perfume (often alcohol-free) mixing oud with florals, spices, or musks. |
| **Attar** | Oil-based perfume traditionally dabbed on skin; many Gulf attars use oud as a base. |
| **Hijazi / Hindi / Cambodi (and similar labels)** | Regional style labels for oud character — useful as a scent map, not a guarantee of exact origin. |
| **Projection** | How far the scent travels from the wearer. |
| **Longevity** | How long the scent remains noticeable on skin or fabric. |
| **Sillage** | The trail left in the air after you walk past. |

## How oud is often worn in the Gulf

- Layer oil on pulse points, then a spray or bakhoor for clothes.
- Prefer lighter or fresher tops in peak heat; richer oud for evening or air-conditioned spaces.
- Fabric (thobe, abaya, scarf) holds scent longer than bare skin in humidity.
